# Prudential Life (PruClimate) Malaria & Cholera Intervention — M&E Dashboard

**United Way Ghana | Chorkor, Accra — Chemuenaa, Lante Maami & Tee Garden communities**

> Monitoring & Evaluation dashboard built from household and school survey data collected for a climate-linked health intervention (malaria and cholera/diarrheal disease prevention, WASH, and sanitation) implemented by United Way Ghana with support from Prudential Life Ghana / PruClimate.

---

## Project Summary

This project tracks the outcomes of a climate action–linked health intervention delivered across three communities in Chorkor, Accra: **Tee Garden, Lante Maami, and Chemuenaa**. The intervention combined toilet facility construction, waste management support, malaria prevention (mosquito nets, health education), and hygiene promotion.

I supported the Monitoring & Evaluation process by helping structure and consolidate household- and school-level survey data — covering demographics, sanitation access, malaria and diarrheal case trends, handwashing practices, water sources, and satisfaction with the project — into a set of Power BI dashboards. The dashboards were built to give the project team a clear, filterable view of progress and remaining gaps across the three communities, to support evidence-based follow-up and reporting.

---

## My Role

- Helped structure raw household and school survey responses (collected via form-based data collection) into clean, analyzable datasets
- Built interactive Power BI dashboards to track sanitation access, malaria/diarrheal case trends, WASH (water, sanitation, hygiene) indicators, and project status by community
- Cross-checked survey figures across communities to flag inconsistencies and data quality issues for follow-up
- Summarized findings into indicators the project team could use to identify implementation gaps and prioritize follow-up action

## Key Findings

Findings below are drawn directly from the 167 household responses and the dashboards above.

**Sanitation & toilet access**
- 89.8% of surveyed households (150/167) reported having access to a toilet facility; 17 households did not.
- Of households without their own toilet, 26% (43/166) reported practicing open defecation.
- Only 31 of 159 households who answered reported that their toilet was built through the Prudential project — most existing toilet access predates the intervention, and 28 households marked the question "not applicable."
- **Project (toilet construction) status was recorded as "not available" for 92 of 167 households (55%)**, with 60 marked "in progress" and only 15 marked "completed" — this is the clearest data gap for follow-up monitoring.

**Waste management**
- Only 31% of households (52/167) reported having access to a waste bin. Open dumping and open defecation were flagged as the top recurring sanitation challenges across all three communities.

**Malaria**
- 53% of households (88/167) reported malaria cases had reduced compared to the previous year; 40% were "not sure," and only 4 households reported an increase — a broadly positive signal, though the high "not sure" share suggests limited household-level tracking of trends.

**Diarrheal / cholera-related illness**
- 45% (75/167) reported diarrheal cases had reduced year-on-year; 49% were "not sure."
- Only 35% of households (58/167) treat their drinking water before use — a residual risk area given the project's cholera-prevention focus.

**Community perception**
- 97% of households (160/165) believe the project has improved sanitation in their community.
- Satisfaction was largely positive: 55% satisfied, 16% very satisfied, 29% neutral, and 1 household dissatisfied.

**School-level data (Tee Garden, n=21)**
- 15 of 20 responding schools said the project had improved student attendance; 13 of 20 said malaria-related absenteeism had reduced compared to the previous year.
- Soap availability at handwashing stations was inconsistent — several schools reported soap was only "sometimes" available rather than always.

## M&E Approach

- **Data collection:** Structured household and school survey forms covering demographics, sanitation access, WASH practices, malaria and diarrheal case history, and project satisfaction
- **Data consolidation:** Responses organized by community into a shared dataset for comparability
- **Data verification:** Cross-checking of figures across community sheets and dashboard views to catch inconsistencies (e.g. mismatched totals) before reporting
- **Visualization:** Interactive Power BI dashboards with community-level filtering, so the same set of indicators can be viewed in aggregate or drilled into a single community
- **Reporting:** Summarized indicators intended to help the project team identify implementation gaps (e.g. toilet completion status, waste bin coverage, water treatment) and prioritize follow-up

---

## Recommendations

1. **Close the project-status data gap.** Over half of households have no recorded toilet construction status. Standardize a completion tracker (location, stage, outstanding work, responsible party, follow-up date) so this can't remain "not available."
2. **Expand waste bin coverage.** With only 31% of households having a waste bin, this is a clear driver of the open dumping reported as a top sanitation challenge — prioritize distribution alongside waste collection scheduling.
3. **Promote household water treatment.** Only a third of households treat drinking water before use; pair this with the project's cholera-prevention messaging.
4. **Clarify toilet attribution and ownership.** Many households with toilet access did not have it built through the project — worth confirming this is understood correctly by field teams so project impact isn't over- or under-stated.
5. **Extend school-level monitoring to Chemuenaa and Lante Maami.** The school survey currently only covers Tee Garden; comparable school data from the other two communities would give a fuller picture of the intervention's reach.
6. **Improve household-level trend awareness.** A large share of households answered "not sure" on whether malaria/diarrheal cases changed year-on-year — simple take-home reference cards or community health worker follow-up could improve the quality of this self-reported data.

---

## Limitations

- Figures reflect self-reported household survey responses at the time of data collection, not independently verified clinical or facility records.
- "Not sure" responses on malaria/diarrheal trends were treated as a distinct category rather than excluded, since they are informative about monitoring gaps in their own right.
- The school-level sample (21 responses, one community) is too small to generalize across all three communities.
- Dashboard KPI cards for "Estimated Population" and "Total Household" reflect project-level reference figures rather than figures derived from the surveyed sample.
